Find early buyers (and spot bots)
Finds who bought a token soonest after launch, then flags which of them look like sniper bots vs. real traders — based on how fast they typically flip a position. A heuristic, not a verdict.

Cross-check two tokens
Find token A's whales, then check which of them also bought token B. Nothing gets saved — one-shot lookup.
Find who deployed a token
Pulls the token's creation transaction and reads who paid for it — usually the deployer's wallet. Less reliable for tokens launched through a program/factory.
Trace a wallet's funding lineage
Follows who a known wallet funded, who those wallets funded, and so on — checking every wallet along the way for a buy of the target token. Only catches on-chain funding — won't find a burner funded straight from a CEX withdrawal.
Score a wallet's trading performance
Reconstructs realized PnL from recent swap history, FIFO cost-basis, denominated in SOL. Only round-trip trades (buy then sell) count — this answers "is this wallet actually good," not just "did they buy something once."
Find wallets with a cross-token track record
Give it several tokens that already pumped — it finds wallets that currently hold more than one of them. Catching one pump could be luck; holding three winners in a row is a real signal.

Set up Telegram alerts (free, takes about 2 minutes):
  1. Open Telegram, search for @BotFather, and start a chat with it.
  2. Send /newbot and follow the prompts (any name works). BotFather replies with a bot token — copy it.
  3. Search for your new bot by the username you gave it, open it, and send it any message (e.g. "hi").
  4. Find your chat ID one of two ways: message @userinfobot and it replies with your ID directly, or visit https://api.telegram.org/bot<TOKEN>/getUpdates in a browser and look for "chat":{"id":...} in the response.
  5. In this Worker's dashboard: Settings → Variables and Secrets → add TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN (from step 2) and TELEGRAM_CHAT_ID (from step 4), both as secrets.
  6. Redeploy. The dot above turns green once it's recognized.
